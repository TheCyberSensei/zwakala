# Project instructions

These instructions apply throughout this repository.

- Read existing project files and applicable instructions before editing.
- For complex changes, explain the plan before implementation. Record whether formal Plan Mode was actually used; do not equate a written plan with that mode.
- Make small, focused, reviewable changes.
- Explain significant architectural decisions and record their rationale in `docs/decisions.md`.
- Use appropriate tests and validation where practical. Report actual results, failures and checks that were not run.
- Distinguish verified evidence, participant observations, assumptions and open research questions. Record sources for factual claims.
- Never fabricate sources, research findings, quotations or test results.
- Avoid unnecessary dependencies and infrastructure.
- Prioritise sound architecture, reliable implementation, meaningful tests, evidence-based research, security and privacy. Investigate competing explanations and contradictory evidence; distinguish frontline observations from direct evidence about customers.
- The 11 November 2026 deadline is not permission to rush or reduce quality. Keep engineering simple without cutting corners. Present genuine scope, quality and time conflicts for the participant's decision.
- The earlier banking-staff survey is a separate exploratory direction, archived outside this repository before removal on 11 November 2026. It did not implement or validate the final capstone. Preserve its historical evidence; do not restore it to the current tree, alter its deployed Google Form or access private responses without a new instruction.
- Preserve respondent privacy. Keep identifying information and private research out of tracked files, logs, examples and test fixtures. Prefer storing private material outside the repository; use synthetic data for validation.
- Never commit credentials, tokens, API keys or private research data. Review intended changes before staging or committing. Do not force-add ignored private material.
- Obtain explicit approval before publishing surveys, sharing data, deleting resources or accessing external accounts.
- Obtain explicit approval before creating remote repositories or pushing repository content.
- Record meaningful development decisions and verification results in `docs/codex-development-log.md`, including dates, tasks, Codex features, files, commands/tools, tests and results, human decisions/approvals, problems/resolutions and relevant commits. Use the participant's Africa/Johannesburg timezone for dates. Never put secrets or respondent data in the log.
- Do not generate original capstone ideas. Idea generation belongs to the participant. Do not assume this research satisfies the capstone. The specific African storytelling brief governs capstone scope; the onboarding deck's broader project examples do not override it.
- ZWAKALA's approved connected reporting-to-response prototype is under `prototype/`. Milestones 1–2 are participant-tested and approved; the approved application baseline is `3feabe7bfa5db0154e571b73963c572a3adc9604`, with subsequent approved branding and visual refinement. Milestone 3 closed without local inference or paid APIs: institution tagging is manual and matching rule-based. AI-assisted association is future work only. The reviewed source was published to `TheCyberSensei/zwakala`; the application itself is local-only. The participant approved Stage B visual refinement, regression verification and a reviewed local commit after passing checks. Do not push these changes or deploy without separate approval. Do not use Impeccable or install replacement design skills for this refinement.
- Prototype work must use clearly labelled fictional data only. Enforce permissions on the server, preserve multiple institution relationships per report and keep institutional relevance separate from responsibility. Community disclosure and institution-specific disclosure are separate choices; permission alone must never publish or deliver a report. No external AI services or credentials in Milestones 1–2. Every sharing action must bind the current exact contributor-approved version and recipients to a recorded moderator decision. Corrections and withdrawal stop further sharing; never claim delivered copies have been recalled. Simulated institutional claims are not verified findings or contributor confirmation. See `prototype/README.md` for local-only accounts, storage limits and acceptance gates.
- Do not submit synthetic responses to the research form. Submission-based tests require a separately approved disposable form. Never clear stored creation state, delete items, or create replacement forms automatically after failure.
- Keep the research and final capstone selection separate until the participant explicitly decides otherwise. Preserve human oversight of important technical and research decisions.
- Preserve existing Git history and author metadata. The participant authorises the verified Stage B local commit but has not authorised its push. Do not modify global Git configuration without explicit approval.
